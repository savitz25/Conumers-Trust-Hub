---

## 1. Purpose

Establish the TrustHub network as **credible infrastructure** for independent consumer-protection, regulatory-data, entity-resolution, and public-policy research.

The program may eventually support capstones, practicums, independent research, papers, data-science benchmarking, and public-interest analysis.

**Not claimed in 001A:** university partnerships, current pilots in the field, academic validation, dataset downloads, DOIs, or funded grants.

---

## 2. Independence charter

The public page publishes the same ten principles implemented in `lib/academic/charter.ts`. They are program rules, not decoration:

1. Unfavorable findings are allowed.
2. TrustHub does not require favorable conclusions.
3. Factual correction is allowed; suppression because findings are unfavorable is not.
4. Methodology critique is treated as a chance to improve the system.
5. No consumer PII is provided through the academic program.
6. Limitations and missing evidence must be documented.
7. Original regulators remain the source of the records.
8. Researchers retain independence over analysis and publication.
9. A future limited factual/legal review window is not approval based on favorability.
10. Meaningful methodology changes that follow independent research should be documented publicly when appropriate.

## 6. Success metrics

Partnership count is **not** the primary metric.

Prioritize:

- Completed citable research outputs
- Dataset citations and, later, DOI citations
- Downloads only after an open release exists
- Returning faculty / programs
- Methodology improvements triggered by outside research
- Replicated analyses
- Student recruiting outcomes (secondary)
- High-quality references from researchers, regulators, or journalists

## 8. Counsel review before releases or university agreements

The following require **qualified counsel review**. This file does not provide legal conclusions.

- Dataset licensing and redistribution rights
- Source-data terms of use (FMCSA, NMLS, CMS, SEC, state boards, DOI/NAIC, and others)
- Named-business analysis and defamation risk
- Tortious-interference risk
- Right to a limited factual/legal review without control over findings
- Controlled versus open datasets
- Consumer PII prohibition and business de-identification where appropriate
- Potential IRB requirements for human-subjects work
- Publication disclaimers
- FCRA-adjacent considerations for lending-related extracts
- Public-record provenance and attribution requirements

See `docs/ACADEMIC-DATASET-RELEASE-STANDARD.md`.
